Financial Advisory

Financial advisory for business

This is corporate financial advice for companies and the people who own them. It is not personal wealth management, it is not pensions advice for individuals, and it is not a sales desk for financial products. If you have arrived looking for an adviser to manage your savings, this is the wrong page. If you run a business and need specialist financial input on a deal, a dispute, a valuation or a funding structure, this is the right one.

Book a free, confidential call

Where this fits

Most businesses meet a firm like ours at one of two moments. The first is when something has gone wrong and they need restructuring or insolvency advice. The second is when something significant is being planned or contested, and the numbers need an independent specialist rather than an in-house view. This section is about the second.

The connection between the two is real and worth stating. The same judgement that reads a company's finances in a crisis reads them just as well before a sale, during a dispute, or when a lender wants an independent set of eyes. We work alongside your existing accountant and solicitor, and we are brought in for the specific question rather than to replace anyone.

What financial advisory covers

Corporate finance

Advice on raising money, selling a business, buying one, or valuing shares. The work runs from preparing a company for sale through to negotiating the deal and structuring how it is paid for. This is the highest-value service in the section and the one owners most often plan years ahead.

Corporate finance

Forensic services

Independent financial investigation for disputes, litigation and suspected fraud. We quantify loss, trace funds, and produce work that stands up as expert evidence. Solicitors are the usual route in, and the output has to survive cross-examination rather than merely inform.

Forensic services

Pension advisory

Corporate pension questions rather than personal ones: scheme obligations, how a pension sits inside a transaction, and how deficits are handled in a restructuring or a sale. Advice to the business and its directors, not retail pension planning.

Pension advisory

Capital management

Getting the shape of a company's funding right. How much working capital the business actually needs, how the capital structure supports the plan, and where cash is tied up that should not be. Often the quiet difference between a business that copes and one that keeps hitting the same wall.

Capital management

Transaction support

Due diligence and deal support for buyers and sellers. Checking that a target is what it appears to be, or preparing your own numbers so a buyer's adviser finds nothing that stalls the deal. The work that decides whether a transaction completes on the terms agreed.

Transaction support

Commercial arbitration

Independent input where a commercial dispute turns on financial questions. Valuation disagreements, contract disputes and shareholder fallings-out where an objective financial view helps resolve matters without a full court fight.

Commercial arbitration

Who commissions this work

Three groups tend to bring us in, and the reason matters because it shapes the advice.

Owners planning something.
A sale, a succession, an acquisition, or a fundraising. These are the calmest and best-prepared instructions, and the ones where good advice changes the outcome most, because there is time to act on it
Directors under pressure from a specific event.
A dispute, an HMRC enquiry, a lender asking hard questions, a co-owner wanting out. The financial facts need establishing independently before anyone negotiates
Other professionals.
Accountants and solicitors who need a specialist alongside them for a piece of work outside their own remit, and who want someone who will hand the client back rather than keep them

In every case the value is the same: an independent read on the numbers from people who have no product to sell and no incentive to reach a particular answer.

Why independence is the point

A lot of financial advice is attached to something the adviser is selling, whether that is a fund, a loan, or a formal appointment further down the line. Ours is not. We are paid for the advice, and the advice is the whole of it.

That matters most when the honest answer is inconvenient: that a deal is priced wrong, that a dispute is not worth fighting, that a business is worth less than its owner hoped. Advice that costs the adviser nothing to give straight is worth more than advice shaped by what happens next.

How we work with your existing advisers

We are rarely the only firm in the room, and we prefer it that way. Your accountant handles the year-round compliance and knows the business. Your solicitor handles the law. We handle the specific financial question, and we bring in licensed colleagues, valuers or lenders where a particular part of the work needs them.

For funding itself we do not act as a broker. Where a transaction needs new finance we introduce you to a finance and funding specialist we know and trust, and we say plainly when more debt is the wrong answer.
