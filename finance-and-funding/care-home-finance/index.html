Care home finance

Care home funding sits between a commercial mortgage and trading business lending, and lenders treat it as both. The building is substantial security, but its value depends heavily on continuing to operate as a care home, which depends in turn on the operator holding its registration.

What lenders look at

Occupancy first, because it drives everything else in the finance and funding decision. The mix between local authority funded and privately funded residents, since fee rates differ substantially and a home weighted towards local authority rates has thinner margins. Staffing cost and agency dependence, which has been the sector's sharpest pressure. The building itself, including whether the rooms meet current expectations on ensuite provision and size. And the regulatory position, which in Scotland means the Care Inspectorate rather than the Care Quality Commission, a distinction that matters and that English-written guidance gets wrong.

Who it suits, and who it does not

It may suit you if

You are acquiring or refinancing a home with stable occupancy and a defensible fee mix, and the funding is sized against realistic staffing costs rather than best-case ones. Your regulatory grades are sound, because a lender will look at them.

It is the wrong move if

Occupancy is falling, agency dependence is high and rising, or there is an unresolved regulatory issue. In this sector a regulatory problem becomes a financial one very quickly, because it affects admissions, and admissions drive the income that services the debt.

Why regulatory and financial distress arrive together

A care home in financial difficulty tends to reduce spending in ways that show up in inspection: staffing thins, agency use rises, maintenance is deferred. A poorer grade then affects new admissions, occupancy falls, and the financial position worsens. It is a loop, and by the time it is visible in the accounts it has usually been running for a while. It also constrains the options: an insolvency in this sector cannot simply stop trading, because there are residents in the building, and any administration or sale has to be managed with the regulator and the placing local authorities involved from the outset. That is a materially more complex process than an ordinary business sale and it needs more notice, which is the practical reason to take advice early here rather than late.
