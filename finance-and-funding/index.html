Finance & Funding

Business finance and funding

We don't try to do everything. Lancefield is an insolvency practitioner and restructuring specialist, and that's where our advice carries weight. When a client needs commercial finance, we refer to a trusted specialist firm we know well. Not a panel. Not a broker pool. The same integrity-first standard that shapes our advice shapes who we refer to. If we recommend a firm, it's because we'd use them ourselves.

Why we refer instead of sell

A lot of firms that advise businesses in difficulty also earn commission arranging their finance. That arrangement shapes the advice, because a firm paid to place a loan has a reason to recommend one. We took the money out of that decision. We do not broker finance, we hold no lending panel, and no lender pays us to send them your name.

What we do instead is tell you honestly whether finance is the right answer at all, and if it is, introduce you to a specialist we know and rate. Sometimes the honest advice is that more borrowing is the wrong medicine, and because we earn nothing from the loan either way, we can say so plainly. That is the whole point of keeping this at arm's length.

When funding is the right answer

Used well, finance solves real problems. A profitable business can simply be short of working capital. Cash can be tied up in an unpaid sales ledger, in equipment, or in property, when it could be released and put to work. The right facility, matched to the trading cycle, fixes that without changing anything else about the business.

Used badly, finance postpones a problem and adds cost to it. Borrowing to cover a loss that keeps recurring buys a few months and a larger debt. Part of what an honest introduction gives you is someone who knows the difference and will tell you which one you are looking at. Where the underlying issue is structural rather than a cash-flow gap, the answer sits with restructuring rather than a new facility.

The kinds of finance we can point you to

By type

Invoice finance

Releasing cash tied up in unpaid invoices, so money arrives when the work is done rather than when the customer eventually pays.

Invoice finance

Asset finance

Funding against equipment and vehicles, either to buy them or to release the value already sitting in them.

Asset finance

Commercial mortgage

Longer-term borrowing secured on business premises, spreading repayment over a horizon that matches the asset.

Commercial mortgage

Bridging finance

Short-term funding to cover a defined gap, for example between buying and selling, with a clear and realistic exit.

Bridging finance

VAT loans

Spreading a VAT bill over manageable instalments instead of taking a quarterly hit to cash flow.

VAT loans

Unsecured business loans

Borrowing that is not tied to a specific asset, useful for growth or a one-off need where speed matters.

Unsecured business loans

By sector

Some industries fund themselves in particular ways, and a specialist who knows the sector arranges better terms than a generalist. We can introduce you to firms that work in property finance, engineering finance, hospitality finance, contractor finance, sports and entertainment finance and care home finance.
