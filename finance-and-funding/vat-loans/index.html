VAT and tax loans

A VAT loan is short-term borrowing that pays a tax bill on your behalf so you can repay it over several months rather than in one payment. The same product exists for corporation tax and for self-assessment liabilities.

Book a free, confidential call

It is a straightforward facility and it solves a real problem for businesses whose income is lumpy. It is also, more often than the industry admits, an early warning sign, and this page is mainly about telling the two situations apart.

What it does

It converts a single large payment into a series of smaller ones, usually over three to twelve months, at a cost. For a business with genuinely seasonal or contract-driven income, that smoothing is exactly what it should be used for: the money is there across the year, just not on the day the bill falls due. The lender pays HMRC directly, so the liability is settled and you owe the finance company instead.

Who it suits, and who it does not

It may suit you if

Your income is lumpy for a structural reason and the bill has landed in a trough. The business is profitable and the cash arrives, just not in the right week. You are using it occasionally rather than as a matter of course.

It is the wrong move if

You need one every quarter. At that point the business is not experiencing a timing problem, it is trading beyond its working capital, and each quarter's borrowing makes the next one harder. That is a capital management question, and if the trend is downwards it is a turnaround one.

Why we treat repeat tax funding as a warning

VAT is money the business has already collected on HMRC's behalf. If it is not there when the return falls due, it has been used to fund something else, usually the ordinary shortfall between what the business spends and what it collects. Borrowing to replace it fixes the symptom for a quarter. What we look at instead is why the money was not there, because that answer is the one that decides whether the business has a cash timing issue or a solvency issue. There is also a conduct dimension worth knowing: where a company later fails, an insolvency practitioner has to report on the directors' conduct, and using tax money to pay other creditors is one of the things the Insolvency Service names as unfit conduct. Our creditors' voluntary liquidation page covers that.
