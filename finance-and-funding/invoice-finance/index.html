Invoice finance

Invoice finance turns unpaid sales invoices into cash now. A provider advances most of the value of an invoice within a day or two of you raising it, then releases the balance, less its charges, once your customer pays. You are not borrowing against property and you are not selling anything new. You are being paid closer to the day the work was actually done.

Factoring and discounting are not the same thing

The two main forms differ in one respect owners care about, which is who speaks to your customers.

Invoice factoring.
The provider collects. It runs the sales ledger and chases payment directly, so your customers know a third party is involved. For a business with no credit control function that is often a relief rather than an embarrassment
Invoice discounting.
You collect. The facility sits behind the scenes and your customers need never know it exists, which is why it is called confidential. Providers usually want better systems and stronger figures before they offer it, because they are relying on you to do the chasing

Scots law treats the transfer of a debt differently from English law, so a provider may document a Scottish ledger differently. If you invoice on both sides of the border, ask how each is handled before you sign.

Who it suits, and who it does not

It fits a business selling to other businesses on credit terms, billing for work already delivered, with a reasonable spread of solid customers. It is at its best when growth is the problem, because every new order consumes cash before it produces any. That is a working capital question, and capital management sets out the mechanics.

It suits others badly. Selling to consumers, billing in stages before work is done, depending on one customer for most of your turnover, or contracts that bar you from assigning the debt will each narrow or close the door. And if the business loses money on every job, this releases cash faster without fixing anything. Funding equipment and vehicles is a separate question that belongs with asset finance.

The warning nobody gives you

This is the fastest way to release trapped working capital in a business under pressure. It is also the facility most likely to be withdrawn as that pressure worsens. The provider sees your ledger every week, so it notices deterioration before your bank does and usually before you have finished admitting it. Credit notes rise, debts age, one customer fails, and the terms tighten: a lower advance, certain debts excluded, a reserve held back. That is how the product is designed to behave. The trouble is that the cash it takes back is cash you have already spent.

So the timing matters more than the rate. A facility put in while the business is stable is a tool. One reached for in the last quarter before a crisis tends to bring the crisis forward, and a facility pulled at the wrong moment is a well-worn route into administration for a company with a full order book. If you are near that point, the conversation is turnaround or a restructuring plan rather than a new facility, and contingency planning is where we would begin.
