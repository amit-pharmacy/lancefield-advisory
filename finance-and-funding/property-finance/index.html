Finance & Funding

Property finance

Property finance covers two quite different things. Development finance funds a build or a conversion, drawn down in stages as the work progresses and repaid when the scheme is sold or refinanced. Investment finance is longer-term borrowing against property held to let, which is closer to a commercial mortgage.

How development finance works

Unlike the finance and funding products that advance in a single sum, the lender releases development money against a programme. Funds are released in stages as work is completed and signed off, usually by a monitoring surveyor acting for the lender, with interest typically rolled up and repaid at the end out of the sale or refinancing. The whole structure assumes the programme holds: that each stage completes roughly when it was said it would, at roughly the cost stated, and that the exit arrives when planned.

Who it suits, and who it does not

It may suit you if

You have a costed scheme, a contractor, a realistic programme and a credible exit, whether that is a sale or a term refinancing. There is contingency in the numbers for the things that always happen, and you can fund a period of overrun without the scheme stopping.

It is the wrong move if

The contingency is thin, the programme is optimistic, or the exit depends on a market rather than on a buyer. Development schemes rarely fail because the buildings were wrong; they fail because time and cost ran past the funding, and the shortest route to that is a programme with no slack in it.

Where schemes actually go wrong

Cost overruns and delays turn into distress faster in development than anywhere else, because the drawdown schedule assumes the programme and the interest keeps rolling up while nothing is being sold. A three-month delay is not a three-month problem: it is three more months of rolled-up interest, a later exit, and often a contractor who now wants paying differently. The lender's security is the site, and the site is worth considerably less half-finished than finished, which shapes how a lender behaves when a scheme stalls. If yours is slipping, the time to talk to the lender is before the next drawdown request, not after it is declined, and our debt restructuring page covers what lenders will and will not agree to.
