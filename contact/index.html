The form comes straight to us. A sentence or two is enough: who you are, what the business does, and what has brought you here.

How quickly we reply

Enquiries arriving during a working day are answered that day, and anything landing over a weekend is picked up on Monday morning. If a winding up petition has been served, or another clock is already running, say so in the first line.

What the first conversation covers

Around half an hour, by phone, video, or in person in Glasgow. We ask what has happened and where the pressure is coming from, then tell you where you stand.

What is actually wrong.
Distress and insolvency are different states, and the difference decides what is still open to you. Where that is unclear, an independent business review settles it
Which routes are realistic.
Turnaround work, debt restructuring, a company voluntary arrangement, or an orderly liquidation. Each carries consequences and we go through them
Where you stand personally.
A signed personal guarantee makes the company problem and your own finances one problem in two halves. Debt Help Scotland covers that side

Where a formal appointment genuinely is needed, we bring in a licensed insolvency practitioner we know and trust. That is an introduction, not a sale.

What to have ready

Nothing. You do not need figures or a tidy explanation, and plenty of first calls happen with someone who has not opened the post in a fortnight. If you have recent management accounts, a rough list of who is owed what, and the detail of any guarantees you signed, we get specific faster.

What we do with what you tell us

It stays with us. We do not approach your bank, your lender, HMRC or your accountant unless you ask us to, and nothing becomes formal unless you decide to start something. If you would rather we left no voicemail, say so.

If you feel awkward about calling

Almost everyone does. Directors tend to open by apologising, as though two hard years were a character flaw. Businesses run into trouble for ordinary reasons and we are not going to be shocked by yours. The people who leave it longest have the least room left to move, which is the only real cost of waiting. That is the argument for contingency planning.

For accountants and solicitors

If you have a client in difficulty, contact us directly rather than through the form. We take no appointments and do no compliance work, so we are not competing for your relationship. Your client stays yours. We give a second opinion, an options review, or the conversation you would rather not be the one to have. Much of our restructuring and financial advisory work starts this way.
