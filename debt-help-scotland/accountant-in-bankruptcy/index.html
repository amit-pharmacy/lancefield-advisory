Debt help in Scotland

The Accountant in Bankruptcy, explained

The Accountant in Bankruptcy is the public body that runs personal insolvency in Scotland. It awards bankruptcy, acts as trustee in a large share of cases and supervises the trustee in the rest, keeps the public register of everyone in a formal process, and runs the statutory repayment scheme. Most people meet the name on an envelope, and the letter inside rarely explains what the organisation is.

What the Accountant in Bankruptcy is

The AiB, as it is shortened in its own letters and almost everywhere else, is a Scottish Government executive agency: inside government, but at arm's length from ministers, with its own chief executive and its own statutory duties. Most of those duties come from the Bankruptcy (Scotland) Act 2016. The Accountant in Bankruptcy is also a person, a statutory office holder appointed by Scottish Ministers, whose staff work out of Kilwinning in Ayrshire.

It holds two roles that pull against each other. It regulates, overseeing the trustee in every Scottish personal insolvency case and taking complaints about how that trustee has behaved. It also administers cases itself, and in a great many of them it is the trustee. One body can therefore be your case handler, your trustee and the referee at once.

What the AiB actually does

Awards bankruptcy.
Apply for your own bankruptcy and the AiB decides, not a court. Creditors wanting you made bankrupt against your will petition the sheriff court
Acts as trustee.
In a debtor application the AiB becomes trustee over your estate unless a licensed insolvency practitioner is named and agrees to act
Supervises every other trustee.
An insolvency practitioner acting as trustee in a Scottish bankruptcy, or under a protected trust deed, accounts to the AiB for the case
Runs the Debt Arrangement Scheme.
As DAS Administrator it approves each debt payment programme and oversees the money moving through it
Keeps the Register of Insolvencies.
The public record of Scottish personal insolvency cases and of corporate appointments north of the border
Grants moratoriums.
A short application buys a period during which creditors cannot enforce against you while you take advice
Publishes the numbers.
Quarterly Scottish insolvency statistics, and the Notes for Guidance that trustees and money advisers work from

Two of those matter far more than the rest: whether the AiB is your trustee, and what reaches the register.

When the AiB is your trustee

A trustee takes control of your estate, sells what can be sold, deals with creditors' claims and pays out what there is. Somebody does that job in every bankruptcy, and here it falls to the AiB by default.

The AiB acts as trustee wherever nobody else is appointed. In a debtor application you may name an insolvency practitioner willing to act, and that person becomes your trustee instead. It is also trustee in every Minimal Asset Process bankruptcy, and it picks up any case where a trustee resigns, dies or is removed without replacement.

Your trustee is not your adviser and is not acting for you. The duties are owed to creditors, and they take in how the debt arose, what assets you have not mentioned and whether you have income to spare. Being straight from the first form is the most useful thing you can do.

Creditors then claim through the appointment rather than chasing you. Most must stop contacting you directly, and they cannot continue diligence, the Scottish word for enforcement such as an earnings arrestment or a bank arrestment. Creditors keep some rights: a secured lender still holds its security over your home, and creditors can object to your discharge.

Where you have surplus income, it is assessed and you are asked for a monthly payment, set by statutory calculation rather than by negotiation. That payment runs for a fixed period. Benefits count as income in the assessment, which is why people living mainly on benefits are frequently assessed as having nothing to pay.

The Register of Insolvencies

The AiB keeps that register, and it is genuinely public: anyone with an internet connection can search it, free, without saying who they are or why. This is the part most people find hardest, and it is better faced now than discovered later.

An entry names you, gives an address, and records the date of the award, the trustee's name and contact details, and the date you are discharged. It does not show what you owe or to whom. A trust deed appears once it is protected, and Scottish company appointments appear too, which is how creditors check a customer.

Very few people are ever looked up. Credit reference agencies do check, and so do some lenders and some employers in regulated roles. The larger effect is on your credit rating: a bankruptcy sits on your credit file for years counted from the award rather than from your discharge. For most people the credit rating damage arrived long before, with the defaults that led here.

Your bank account is a separate question. Bankruptcy does not close a bank account by itself, but banks often freeze or close one on learning of it, particularly where the same bank is owed money. Opening a basic bank account elsewhere first, with wages and benefits paid into it, saves trouble later.

How the AiB differs from the Insolvency Service

The Insolvency Service is a UK government agency covering England and Wales, and its official receivers act there. It has no role in Scottish personal insolvency, and the AiB has none south of the border. What is an IVA in England is a protected trust deed here, and the statutory repayment scheme has no English counterpart.

Some differences are structural. In England and Wales a debtor's bankruptcy application goes to an adjudicator; here it goes to the AiB, and there is no official receiver because the AiB does that work itself. Nor does the AiB license insolvency practitioners: professional bodies authorise them across the UK, and the AiB supervises how they run Scottish cases.

Corporate insolvency runs the other way round. Liquidation and administration are largely GB-wide law, so a Scottish company closing follows broadly the same statute as an English one, and the AiB appoints nobody to a company. A director facing a failed business and a personal shortfall deals with two systems in the same month.

Dealing with the AiB

Everything runs on your case reference.
Quote it on every letter, form and call
Most dealings are with the trustee, not a switchboard.
Where an insolvency practitioner holds the appointment, the AiB supervises rather than handles the case
Answer the forms fully and on time.
A half complete statement of assets and liabilities invites more questions
Report changes as they happen.
A new job, a drop in income, a move or an inheritance all have to be disclosed, and any payment can be reassessed either way
Ask for a review if a decision looks wrong.
Many AiB decisions can be reviewed on request, with an appeal to the sheriff from the review, and the time limits are short
Complaints have their own route.
The AiB runs a complaints procedure, and what it does not resolve can go to the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman

One thing causes most of the frustration we hear about: the AiB administers the process, it does not advise you on whether the process suits you. Its staff cannot say whether a trust deed would protect your home better than bankruptcy would. The law requires an approved money adviser before any debtor application in any event, and free advice is available from Citizens Advice, StepChange and National Debtline.

Where the AiB fits among your options

Most people who reach this page are working out which of three formal routes applies. The AiB touches all three.

Bankruptcy, or sequestration

The formal name is sequestration, though bankruptcy is the ordinary Scottish word and the AiB uses both. Assets are realised, creditors receive what there is, and the balance is written off. The AiB is either your trustee or the supervisor of the practitioner who is, and under the Minimal Asset Process it is always the trustee.

Protected trust deed

A voluntary arrangement with your creditors, run by an insolvency practitioner and supervised by the AiB rather than administered by it. You pay a contribution for a set period and the remaining unsecured balance is written off at the end, provided the deed became protected and you kept to its terms. Our page on trust deeds explains how protection is obtained.

Debt Arrangement Scheme

A statutory repayment programme: you repay the debt in full over a longer period, interest and charges frozen, creditors unable to enforce, and nothing written off because nothing needs to be. The AiB approves it as DAS Administrator. See Debt Arrangement Scheme.

If the debt is your company's rather than yours, the AiB is not the body you need. A winding up petition already served, a director-led CVL, or a partnership insolvency where partners carry personal liability are corporate questions first. The rest of Debt Help Scotland covers the personal side, and the two are best dealt with together.
