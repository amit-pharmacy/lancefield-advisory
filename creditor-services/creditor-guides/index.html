Creditors' guides to insolvency practitioners' fees

If a company or person that owes you money has entered a formal insolvency procedure, you have a financial interest in what the office holder is paid, because it comes from the same estate your dividend does. The profession publishes a guide to fees for each procedure, and this page points you to the right one.

What SIP 9 asks of an office holder

SIP 9 is the Statement of Insolvency Practice on payments from an estate. The Scottish version took effect on 1 April 2021 and covers proceedings under the Insolvency Act 1986 and the Bankruptcy (Scotland) Act 2016. Solvent cases sit outside it, so a members' voluntary liquidation is caught only if the shareholders ask.

Anyone asked to approve a payment should be given enough to form a view: what work is planned and why, what it should cost, and afterwards what was done and what it came to. The office holder must also point creditors to information about their rights. These guides are that information.

How the fee is calculated

Remuneration is fixed on one of three bases: a percentage of the value of the property the office holder deals with, the work reasonably undertaken by the office holder and staff, which everyone calls time cost, or a set amount. Bases can be combined across different parts of the work.

For a percentage or a set amount, the office holder should explain why it fairly reflects the work and which costs it covers. For time cost, you should see charge out rates by grade of staff. Expenses are approved separately, and any paid to an associate need approving like the fee itself.

How fees are fixed in Scotland

Scotland has its own corporate insolvency rules, so a guide written for England and Wales will send you the wrong way. In an administration, the creditors' committee fixes outlays and remuneration. Where there is no committee, the creditors do it by decision procedure. If they fail to, the claim goes to the court, which must determine it.

In a creditors' voluntary liquidation, or a court winding up that began with a winding up petition, the liquidator submits accounts and a claim after each accounting period, and the liquidation committee audits them and fixes the amount. Where no committee exists, that falls to the court.

Two rights follow. A creditor may appeal a committee determination to the court within 14 days, having first given the liquidator notice. And creditors representing a quarter of the debt by value may apply for an order reducing outlays or remuneration as excessive.

Finding the guide for your procedure

There is a separate guide for each procedure, with Scottish versions where the law differs. Use the one matching your appointment.

Liquidation.
Covers liquidation in each form, creditor led, court led and solvent
Administration.
Including a sale agreed before appointment, a pre-pack administration, which carries further disclosure under SIP 16
Company voluntary arrangement.
In a company voluntary arrangement the nominee's and supervisor's fees usually go to creditors with the proposal
Receivership.
For a fixed charge appointment over property, see LPA receivership
Personal insolvency.
A trustee's fees in a sequestration or protected trust deed fall under the Bankruptcy (Scotland) Act 2016, fixed by the commissioners or the Accountant in Bankruptcy
Partnerships.
Where the debtor is a firm rather than a company, see partnership liquidation
