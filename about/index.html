About Lancefield Advisory

Lancefield Advisory is an insolvency and restructuring advisory firm in Glasgow. We advise company directors, sole traders, partners and individuals under financial pressure who want to know where they actually stand before committing to anything.

Book a free, confidential call

Why the firm exists

The insolvency and debt advice market has a trust problem, and it is no secret inside the profession. Much of the advice comes from people paid out of the procedure they recommend, whether from the appointment itself or from a product carrying a commission. So the question of whether you need that procedure gets answered by someone with a stake in the answer.

The pattern is familiar. Liquidation proposed where a turnaround was still possible, or a director pushed towards a default answer before anyone has looked at what they signed personally. The harm is rarely a shortage of options. It is the absence of an unbiased read on them while there is still time to use them.

Lancefield was set up as the counterweight. We look at the whole position, business and personal together, and say what we think.

Phil Dunn

Lancefield Advisory is led by Phil Dunn, who has spent over twenty years in restructuring and recovery, gained across three of the Big Four accounting firms. That is two decades at the difficult end of this work: lenders who have run out of patience, HMRC arrears that have been rolling for a year or more, and directors who signed a personal guarantee and have not slept properly since.

Phil works as a consultant rather than as a licensed insolvency practitioner, and the distinction is deliberate rather than incidental. He is not licensed to take formal appointments and does not seek them. What he does is establish where a business or an individual actually stands, then bring in the right licensed specialist for whichever part of the problem needs one. On any given case that might be an insolvency practitioner, a solicitor, a valuer, a tax adviser or a funder. His job is to work out what is genuinely required and assemble it, not to be all of it.

He describes his own role as a trusted adviser rather than a technician, and the test he applies to it is a practical one: whether a professional contact sends him a second client after the first. Most of his work arrives that way.

Who sends us work, and why

A large share of what we do comes from other professionals rather than directly from the businesses themselves, and the reasons are worth stating because they explain how we work.

Accountants
who cannot carry out restructuring or debt work themselves, whether for independence reasons, because the work sits outside their practice, or because the client relationship is too valuable to risk on unfamiliar ground
Solicitors
approached by a client for corporate debt advice, who need a financial specialist alongside them rather than a firm that will absorb the client
Lenders and funders
who need an independent read on a borrower's position before deciding what to do about it
Directors and individuals
who found us directly, usually late at night, usually after months of not asking anyone

What the professional referrers want is the same thing in each case: a specialist who will handle a sensitive situation properly, keep them informed, and hand the client back. That is the whole basis of the relationship and it is why the independent business review and advisory work sits where it does, deliberately separate from taking appointments.

Who we work with

Distressed small and medium-sized businesses, sole traders, partners and private individuals. In practice that means companies from around £50,000 of turnover up to roughly £20 million, which is the range where the owner is still in the room and the decisions are personal as well as commercial.

We advise across every sector, though three come through the door more than the rest: hospitality and leisure, construction, and the supply chain behind both. That concentration is useful to a client, because it means we can usually tell you in a first conversation whether what you are seeing is ordinary for your sector or a sign of something structural. Our hospitality finance and contractor finance pages set out what we see in each.

Geographically we are a Glasgow firm, and the people we work with cover Scotland, England and Ireland. The jurisdictions genuinely differ, which matters more than most advisers admit. Personal insolvency in Scotland runs on its own statute with its own regulator, and a firm working from English precedent will get parts of it wrong. Our Debt Help Scotland section covers sequestration, trust deeds and the Debt Arrangement Scheme as they actually work here.

What a good outcome looks like

There is a persistent misconception about why businesses fail and what happens when they get into difficulty, and it costs people time they cannot afford. Insolvency is treated as a single event with one meaning, when the reality is a range of outcomes that differ enormously.

The part that gets least attention is the positive side of a restructuring process: jobs kept, suppliers paid, a landlord dealt with properly, a business that continues in some form rather than stopping. Those outcomes are frequently available and frequently missed, because the people who could have taken them did not know they were there.

So the most satisfying work here is rarely the dramatic kind. It is the case where someone asked early enough that the good options were still on the table.

Why we take no appointments

Certain roles in a formal insolvency can only be held by a licensed insolvency practitioner: liquidator, administrator, nominee and supervisor of a company voluntary arrangement, and in Scotland trustee in a sequestration or under a protected trust deed. Lancefield holds none and does not seek them.

Where a formal appointment genuinely is needed, we bring in a licensed practitioner we know and trust, and stay alongside you rather than handing you over at the door. The same applies to the other specialisms a difficult case pulls in: valuers, solicitors, tax advisers, funders. Phil's job is to work out what is actually required and assemble it, not to be all of it.

Because nobody here takes the appointment, we earn nothing from one happening, which leaves us free to say when a formal procedure is the wrong answer. Most firms in this market are not in a position to write that sentence.

What we believe

Six principles run through the work. They were written down before this website was, and they decide the awkward cases.

Integrity first.
The advice has to be right for the client, including when it is uncomfortable or less profitable for us
Clarity over complexity.
Confusion does real damage here, and plain explanation is most of what restores control
People before profit.
Vulnerable situations call for restraint, not a sales process
Results through realism.
Honest outcomes rather than false hope, and no promises about what a procedure will achieve
Follow-through.
Responsibility does not end when the advice is given, it runs through to resolution
Specialism over breadth.
We do not try to do everything. Where a specialist firm will serve you better, we refer, and we stand behind who we refer to

That last one is why our finance and funding and accountancy pages point to a trusted specialist firm rather than pretend the work happens in house. Not a panel. Not a broker pool.

The Clarity Path

People hesitate because they think asking a question starts something they cannot stop. It does not. You keep control of the pace.

Confidential consultation.
A first conversation to understand the situation and any deadlines already running against you. Free, and it commits you to nothing
Diagnostic review.
The full picture, business and personal at once, so nothing surfaces late. Where a lender or board needs it in writing, that becomes an independent business review
Clear, unbiased options.
Every realistic route in plain English with its consequences attached, from turnaround and debt restructuring through to liquidation where that is where things stand
Supported implementation.
If action is needed we handle the mechanics, deal with creditors and HMRC, and bring in a licensed practitioner where the law requires one
Ongoing support.
We stay involved after the decision, through recovery and onto steadier ground

Acting before you are forced to is what most changes the options left, which is what contingency planning is for.

One practical point about how we work. Seeking advice about debt is stressful, and for a lot of people an email form is the wrong way to start. We try to be easy to reach and we still do a great deal of this face to face, because a fair number of clients prefer it to a screen. Whichever you want is fine, and you can contact us either way.

Where we work, and what we cover

We are at 48 West George Street in Glasgow, and work across Scotland, England and Ireland, so where the business sits is rarely the obstacle people expect. The jurisdictions do differ, though. Personal insolvency in Scotland has its own statute and its own regulator, and a firm working from English precedent will get parts of it wrong. Our Debt Help Scotland section covers those routes properly.

The work divides into four areas: restructuring, what a business does while it still has choices; insolvency, the formal procedures including administration; Debt Help Scotland, the personal side, frequently the same problem wearing different clothes; and financial advisory, covering valuation, forensic and transaction work.

Some prefer to sit across a table, others the phone. Either works, so contact us.
