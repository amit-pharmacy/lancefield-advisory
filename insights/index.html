Insights

Plain writing on the decisions directors actually face

This is where we publish what we learn advising Scottish businesses in difficulty. Every piece starts with a question a director or an adviser has put to us, and answers it plainly.

Book a free, confidential call

We do not run commentary on the week's news. Quarterly statistics and court rulings are covered well elsewhere and rarely change what you should do on Monday morning. What is harder to find is a straight account of how a process runs in Scotland, what it means for you personally, and what the alternatives were before anyone reached for a formal appointment.

What you will find here

Three kinds of writing, all of it drawn from live work rather than from a textbook.

How a process actually runs.
Step by step accounts of liquidation, administration and what follows a winding up petition, with Scottish procedure stated correctly rather than borrowed from English practice
The decisions before the process.
Where turnaround work, debt restructuring or an independent business review still leaves options open, and how to tell when it does not
The personal side.
Personal guarantees, director exposure, and the point at which a company problem becomes a sequestration or trust deeds question, which our Debt Help Scotland section covers in full

Because we take no insolvency appointments, we can write about when a formal procedure is the wrong answer. A firm whose fee depends on that procedure happening cannot really write that piece.

Who it is written for

Owners and directors of Scottish businesses first. Accountants and solicitors second, because they are usually asked the question before we are, and something they can forward to a client is more use than a brochure.

If your position is urgent, do not wait for us to write about it. The insolvency and restructuring sections cover the main routes today, and you can contact us for a confidential conversation.
