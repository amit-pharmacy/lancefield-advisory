What a Glasgow accountant actually does for you

Most businesses need an accountant for the steady, essential work that keeps them compliant and informed. In Glasgow, as anywhere else, that means much the same set of services, and it is worth knowing what you are buying before you choose a firm.

Year-end accounts and corporation tax
for your limited company, filed with Companies House and HMRC on time
Bookkeeping and VAT returns
keeping the day-to-day accounting records straight and the quarterly VAT filed correctly
Payroll
so staff and HMRC are paid the right amount on the right date
Self assessment
for directors, sole traders and partners
Management accounts
the monthly or quarterly numbers that tell you how the business is actually doing rather than how it did last year
Audit
for the larger companies and charities that need one

Good accountants offer more than a filing service. They tell you what the numbers mean, flag a problem while it is still small, and answer the question before you have to ask it. That relationship is worth choosing carefully, which is why we only refer to a firm we would use ourselves.

Choosing an accountant in Glasgow

A few things separate accountants who file your accounts from those who genuinely help the business.

They explain, rather than just submit.
You should understand your own numbers after a conversation with your accountant, not feel more confused
They are proactive on tax.
Good advice arrives before the year end, when something can still be done, not after it when nothing can
They have experience with businesses like yours.
A practice built around large corporates often serves a small owner-managed Glasgow business poorly, and the reverse is also true. The same test applies to charities, which are best served by a firm with charity experience
They tell you when something is wrong.
The most valuable accountant is the one who says the difficult thing early, which is exactly the standard we hold ourselves to

Where accountancy meets the work we do

There is a point where accountancy shades into our territory, and knowing it is useful whether you are our client or not. It is the point where a business problem stops being an accounting problem and becomes a solvency one.

When a business starts missing payments, when HMRC arrears build, when new finance is hard to raise, or when a director begins worrying about personal guarantees, the question moves beyond routine accountancy. That is the crossover we specialise in. The problem a set of management accounts shows early might later call for a company voluntary arrangement, a formal liquidation, or, where the pressure has reached the director personally, debt help in Scotland. An unpaid tax bill that becomes a winding up petition is far easier to handle months before it is filed than in the week it lands.

Many of our clients arrive through their own accountant, who recognised the warning signs and wanted a restructuring or insolvency specialist alongside them rather than in place of them.

We work with accountants, not against them. If your accountant refers you to us, we handle the specific insolvency or restructuring question and hand you back. If you come to us directly, we keep your accountant in the loop, because they know the business and we do not intend to replace them. For businesses and their owners we also advise in our own name on financial advisory matters such as valuations, disputes and corporate finance transactions.

If you want to understand how we work before picking up the phone, our about page sets out who we are and the standard we hold ourselves to. When you are ready, contact us and the first conversation is free.
